# T30A: Murage for Chrome presence variants (pick one)

Start with the contact sheets: `shots/sheet-<frame|rail|badge>-<dark|light>.png`. Each shows idle, working, your turn, done, Full permissive, reduced motion, forced colours (high contrast), a dark page, and the five side panel states. Individual files: `shots/<variant>-<theme>-<NN-state>.png` (1280x800 on T15 fixture pages) and `shots/<variant>-<theme>-panel-<state>.png` (390 wide). "dark" and "light" are the Murage theme tokens from `src/styles.css`; the page underneath is the fixture page.

Shared across all three: the cue is hard edged (no glow, no blur, no soft warm edge). The pointer carries the bot's own identity, a teal avatar chip with the initial and name (the product accent is orange, the bot is teal from the Ember palette, so "Murage" and "which bot" never read as one colour). Every on-page shape has a near-black keyline so it reads on white and on black pages. Pause and Stop use the app's button shapes (8px radius, hairline, danger fill for Stop). The side panel is the app's own components: Inter, `--panel` header with the Murage mark, 12px cards with hairlines, mode segmented control, task grants with Revoke, activity list, Continue and Stop task in "Your turn". Only the on-page cue and the state card motif differ per variant.

## A Frame
A 2px accent hairline inset 6px from the viewport edge, with four thick rounded-corner brackets, a control pill at bottom centre, and an arrow pointer with name chip. Waiting keeps only the brackets (no hairline) and the pill turns to Your turn with Continue and Stop task; done turns the brackets green. Full permissive turns the hairline amber and adds a "Full permissive" tag. Side panel: the state card gets matching corner brackets. Not a Claude replica: a thin framed line with corner brackets reads as a viewfinder, not a soft orange glow hugging the edge, and the bot's identity sits on the pointer and the pill.

## B Rail
A solid 46px accent bar across the top of the page holding the bot chip, a status sentence, Pause and Stop, plus a thin sweeping line under it. Pointer is a small accent dot with a pulse ring and the name chip. Waiting flips the rail to ink (clearly different from working), done to green, Full permissive adds an amber and black hazard stripe. Side panel: each state card gets a matching coloured strip. Not a Claude replica: no outline or glow around the viewport at all; the cue is a single flat bar, and the controls live in the page chrome area. Trade-off: it takes 46px at the top of the page and can overlap sticky headers (it floats above them, it does not push the page).

## C Badge
No edge cue except while acting: a dashed accent inset outline that marches slowly (static under reduced motion). A rounded card in the bottom-right corner carries the avatar, the status, and icon-only Pause and Stop. Pointer is a large teal avatar chip with a short fading trail (hidden under reduced motion). Waiting drops the dashed outline and the card gets a solid accent border with Continue and Stop task. Full permissive adds an amber band on the card and an amber dashed outline. Side panel: the state card gets the same dashed or solid border. Not a Claude replica: dashed instead of a glowing line, the card sits in a corner, and the pointer is an avatar, not a cursor. Trade-off: the bottom-right card can cover page content (it will need to move away from a target, as the T14 pill does).

## Accessibility (measured, `scripts/sidepanel-mock/tokens.mjs`, asserted by node --test)
Text pairs need 4.5, non-text 3. Dark / light: text on panel 16.44 / 17.05; secondary text on card 6.93 / 7.46; accent ink on accent fill 6.23 / 5.27; success ink on success fill 8.65 / 5.35; Stop (danger ink on danger) 6.68 / 6.54; Full marker (warning ink on warning) 10.43 / 5.54; bot initial on teal 5.37; Your turn rail text 18.16 / 18.14; accent cue on keyline 6.98 / 3.76; keyline against a white page 19.8. A bright accent line on a pure white page is only 2.84:1 (dark theme) on its own, so every cue carries the keyline outside it; that is why the edge cues read on white.
Reduced motion: the Frame pulse, Rail sweep and ripple, and Badge march and trail all stop (shot `06-working-reduced-motion`); the pointer jumps instead of gliding in the real overlay.
High contrast (forced-colors): cues fall back to system Highlight and CanvasText with 2px borders (shot `07-working-forced-colors`). Frame and Badge hold up. The Rail fallback is the weakest and would need a pass if Rail wins.
State is never colour alone: every state has words ("working", "Your turn", "done", "Full permissive") and a different shape (hairline, bar, border style).
