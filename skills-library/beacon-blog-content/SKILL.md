# blog-content

Plan and run a blog that earns attention and turns readers into subscribers and customers.

## When to use

Use when a business wants a blog, has one that no longer gets read, or asks how often to publish and what to write about.

Trigger phrases:

- "Should I start a blog?"
- "What should I write about?"
- "How often should I post?"
- "My blog gets no traffic."

Do not use to plan keyword research and page briefs in depth (beacon-seo-organic), to run a recurring email (beacon-newsletter) or to plan social posts (beacon-channel-strategy). A blog is slow. If the business needs sales this month, start with beacon-paid-acquisition or beacon-email-sequences.

## Inputs to ask for

1. The buyer: who reads, what they are trying to do, what they search or ask.
2. What the business knows that others do not: customer data, case work, process, opinion.
3. Hours per week available and who writes.
4. Where readers will come from: search, email list, social, partners.
5. The action a reader should take after reading: subscribe, book a call, try the product.

## Procedure

**1. Give the blog one job.** Pick one: win search traffic, support the sales process, build authority, or hold a community. A blog with several jobs usually does none. Write the job in one sentence.

**2. Choose three to five themes.** Each theme is a problem your buyer has where you have first-hand experience. Drop themes where you can only restate what is already online.

**3. Prefer fewer, deeper posts.** Since AI summaries now answer simple questions on the results page, a short explainer rarely earns a click. Posts that earn clicks and citations carry something a summary cannot: original data, a real example, a tested process, a named expert opinion. Google's own guidance asks whether a page offers original information or analysis, and whether a reader can see who wrote it and why.

**4. Set a cadence you can keep for a year.** Common range: one strong post every one to two weeks. Two thorough posts a month beat eight thin ones. Put dates in a calendar and plan eight posts ahead.

**5. Build each post to a standard.**
- Working headline that names the reader and the outcome.
- First 100 words state the answer or promise.
- Author name, role and a line on why they know this.
- One original element: data, screenshot, quote from a customer, a worked example.
- Clear subheads so a skimmer gets the point.
- One call to action that matches the reader's stage, often an email signup.
- Internal links to two related posts.

**6. Plan distribution before writing.** For every post, list where it goes on day one: the email list, one social channel, partners who might share it. Repurpose into a short video, a LinkedIn post or a newsletter section (see beacon-short-form-video, beacon-linkedin, beacon-newsletter).

**7. Refresh, do not just add.** Every quarter, find posts that slipped in traffic. Update with new facts and a real revision note. Changing only the date to look fresh is against Google's guidance.

## Checklist before publishing

- Does it offer something a summary cannot?
- Is the author visible and real? Is any AI help disclosed where it matters?
- Are all claims and numbers sourced or first-hand?
- Does it have one next step?
- Is it mobile readable, with fast load and compressed images?

## Metrics and what good looks like

- Email signups per 1,000 visits: 5 to 20 is a reasonable range for posts with a strong offer.
- Engaged time and scroll depth: look at the trend per post rather than a universal target.
- Share of traffic from search, email and direct: a blog that depends on one source is fragile.
- Impressions and clicks in search reports, and whether the brand shows up in AI answers for your main questions.
- Posts that lead to a sale or call within 30 days, by first or assisted touch.

Expect three to six months before search traffic compounds. Judge early weeks by email signups and reader replies.

## Failure modes

- Publishing on a schedule with nothing to say.
- Writing only what search tools suggest, with no point of view.
- Long posts with no call to action.
- Mass AI drafts with no human edit, no author and no first-hand detail.
- Ignoring old posts that could be refreshed.
- Treating pageviews as the goal rather than subscribers.
