# podcast

Choose the right podcast play, then plan it end to end.

## When to use

Use when a business is thinking of starting a show, wants to be a guest on other shows, or is weighing sponsorship, and when an existing show is not bringing in listeners or leads.

Trigger phrases:

- "Should I start a podcast?"
- "How do I get booked as a guest?"
- "Is podcast sponsorship worth it?"
- "How do I grow my podcast?"

Do not use for short clips alone (beacon-short-form-video) or for a written email series (beacon-newsletter). A solo show is a long commitment. If the business cannot publish for six months, guest first.

## Inputs to ask for

1. The buyer and where they already listen or watch.
2. Time and budget for recording, editing and promotion.
3. Whether the owner is comfortable on camera and in conversation.
4. The outcome: leads, authority, hiring, community, or direct sales.
5. Any existing list or audience to launch to.

## Procedure

**1. Choose the play.**
- *Guest on other shows:* fastest route to a warm audience for a founder or expert. Lowest cost. Start here.
- *Host your own show:* builds authority and relationships with guests who may become partners or buyers. Slow to grow.
- *Sponsor shows:* quick reach with a defined audience. Test small, track by unique link or code.

**2. If hosting, define the show on a page.** Who it is for, the promise, the format, the length, the release day. Interview shows suit relationship building. Solo or two-host shows suit teaching. Choose episode length by the listener's routine, then keep it steady.

**3. Record for video as well as audio.** In 2026 YouTube is the most-used podcast platform in the US and video shows keep more of their audience. Record the conversation on camera, publish the full episode on YouTube, publish audio through your podcast host to Apple Podcasts, Spotify and others, and cut short clips for social.

**4. Set up the basics.** A podcast host with a standard feed, clear cover art, a trailer, three episodes ready at launch, show notes with links and a transcript. Use decent microphones; audio quality matters more than video.

**5. Run a weekly workflow.** Book guests four to six weeks ahead, record in batches, edit, publish, then promote for two weeks: email the list, post three clips, write a short summary post, and ask the guest to share.

**6. If guesting, pitch properly.**
- Research the show: listen to two episodes and check audience size.
- Pitch in five lines: who you are, the topic, why this audience cares, two proof points, a suggested title.
- Offer one tangible takeaway and a landing page made for that show's listeners.
- Follow up once after a week.

**7. If sponsoring, test small.** Buy one or two spots on shows with matching listeners, give each a unique link or code, and compare cost per lead across shows. Host-read ads usually perform better than inserted ones.

## Pitch template

"Hi [name], I enjoyed your episode on [topic]. I help [audience] [outcome] and can talk about [angle] with [proof]. Listeners could leave with [takeaway]. Happy to send a one-page outline."

## Metrics and what good looks like

- Downloads or views per episode in the first 30 days. A new show often lands in the low hundreds; use your own trend.
- Completion: roughly half is typical, 70 percent or more is strong.
- Followers or subscribers gained per episode.
- Landing-page visits and signups by unique link, per show or guest spot.
- Guest-sourced leads or partner introductions, where guests are chosen as prospects.
- Cost per lead for sponsorships.

Podcast apps report plays and listeners by platform, but not who bought. Use one link and one code per show, plus a "how did you hear about us" field at signup.

## Failure modes

- Launching without an audience plan or a promise.
- Stopping after seven episodes. Plan a season length of ten to twenty.
- Guests picked for fame rather than fit.
- Poor audio.
- No call to action, no landing page and no way to measure.
- Sponsoring broad shows with no tracking.
- Skipping video and clips.
