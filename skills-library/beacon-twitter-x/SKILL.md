# twitter-x

Plan an X (formerly Twitter) presence for reach, relationships and real-time category visibility.

## When to use

Use when X is on the channel mix: founder or personal brands, B2B, tech, media, creators, or any business whose audience talks about the category live.

Trigger phrases:

- "How do I grow on X?"
- "Should I be posting on Twitter?"
- "How do I write a good thread?"
- "Do I need X Premium?"

Do not use when the audience is not on X, when no one can post and reply several times a week, or for paid campaigns (beacon-paid-acquisition). Compare against beacon-linkedin for a B2B buyer and beacon-short-form-video for visual reach.

## Inputs to ask for

1. The account: personal, brand, or both.
2. Topics the owner can speak about with real experience.
3. Existing following and who the target readers are.
4. Time per day, and the posting tool in use.
5. The outcome: leads, hires, partners, product feedback, press.

## Procedure

**1. Choose the account and the role.** People follow people. A founder or expert account usually outperforms a brand account. Brand accounts work for support and announcements. Decide the role in one sentence: "I help [reader] with [topic] by posting [format]."

**2. Optimize the profile.** Clear photo, a bio that states who you help and how, a pinned post that shows the best idea or offer, and one link.

**3. Set the post mix.** A workable weekly plan:
- Three to five short posts: one idea each, hook then payoff.
- One or two threads or long posts: a framework, a story, or a teardown, with a strong first line.
- Daily replies: ten to twenty thoughtful replies on posts from larger accounts in your topic.
- One post that invites replies, such as a question or a specific opinion.

**4. Understand what the ranking rewards.** X published its For You ranking code in 2026. It predicts reactions such as likes, replies, reposts, profile clicks, link clicks, dwell time and video views, and also predicts negative reactions such as mute, block, report and not interested. Practical consequences:
- Conversation is weighted most. Replies, and replies you answer, count for more than likes.
- Posts that make readers stop and read through earn dwell time.
- Posts that provoke mutes or reports are penalized.
- Links in the main post tend to reduce reach. Put the link in a reply or the profile.
- Subscribers to the paid tier often see higher reach, but it does not replace good posts. Test it for a month if posting is a core channel, and judge by profile visits and replies.

**5. Write each post to a standard.**
- First line carries the hook: a result, a contrarian point, or a specific number.
- One idea. Cut filler.
- Concrete over general: a named example, a number, a screenshot.
- End with a point or a question. Avoid asking for likes.
- Threads: state the payoff in the first post, keep each post to one step, close with a summary and a pointer.

**6. Reply well.** Be early on rising posts in your topic. Add a fact, an example or a respectful counterpoint. Avoid "great post" and self-promotion. Answer every reply to your own posts within the first 30 minutes where possible.

**7. Turn attention into relationships.** Move good conversations to DMs, keep a list of 30 people you want to know, and post an offer or a signup link once or twice a week.

**8. Review every two weeks.** Look at your top and bottom five posts, find the pattern, and repeat the winners.

## Metrics and what good looks like

- Impressions on posts and replies; track the median, not the best post.
- Profile visits and follower growth from people in your target group.
- Replies per post and the share you answer.
- DM conversations started and meetings booked.
- Clicks on the pinned link and email signups.
- Mute and block signals as a quality check. If growth is fast but replies are hostile, the mix is wrong.

## Failure modes

- Posting only links.
- Broadcasting with no replies.
- Engagement bait, pods and automated replies.
- Rage content that attracts the wrong audience.
- Copying other accounts' posts without adding anything.
- Inconsistent posting, then quitting at week three.
- Measuring likes instead of profile visits and DMs.
