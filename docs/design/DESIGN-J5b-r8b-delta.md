# J5b r8b delta

This addendum supersedes the affected r8 paragraphs. The MAIN rulings remain binding. The build bases are home `2b4d2c4ba1` and CP `94e3e31318`. Migrations remain FILE-ONLY.

1. **Medium 2, independent intake and the publication race.** Intake continues probing and admitting during a slow CP request. Receipt acceptance and admission share a short lock. Acceptance changes the ACK generation and fences admission until that receipt is published. A response captured before that generation change is discarded, even if publication finishes before the response arrives. Probes continue during publication stalls. Positive degradation observations still feed the hold episode; a zero from a stale response cannot complete it. A failed publication does not clear the fence. Publication advances the generation after the file write too, so a probe begun during that write is also stale. An older concurrent publication cannot clear a newer receipt's fence.

   The r8 assertion that every take after CP acceptance follows the app's retirement read is withdrawn. Before that read, either control has no durable receipt and cannot retire other app entries through durable ACKs without also persisting K, or a successful control append publishes K along with those entries. Until K is read as retiring, the app has at most 31 other restore entries beside K; an app overflow that removes K durably ends W. After that read, rule P applies. Stale responses cannot bridge the receipt publication. This supplies the missing transition argument without stopping intake for the duration of the CP call.

2. **Medium 4 and review lines 56-57, recovery and restart.** A hold start and its completion are distinct journal states: `hold` with the episode number, and `hold` with that number and `end:true`. Compaction keeps the latest state. The built r8 already encoded both. R8b also waits for the completion append to succeed before closing the episode in memory. A positive probe cancels an end that has not committed.

   The app cannot recover a failed retirement from an `add` alone. It therefore treats every reloaded live add as unresolved, serves it for control reconciliation, and counts it as degraded. New adds cannot pass these entries until their receipts return. Once a receipt returns, the next successful record includes the owed `done` before new adds. An empty ACK after restart cannot produce a false zero, and cannot allow a later append to bypass K. This conservative restart hold lasts until control reconciliation, including during mixed-version startup. With a working control journal, an unresolved retirement window cannot close and restart its hold episode, so at most one new hold is generated in that window.

3. **Medium 5, rejected admissions and customer recovery.** The rejected-admission count describes unresolved storage failure, not a permanent customer warning. On recovery, the app first commits a fresh failure report for those rejected admissions, then clears their contribution to `opsAlertsWaiting`. Existing durable entries need not retire before the line clears. Retiring or unwritten entries still contribute until resolved. The exact customer sentence is unchanged.

4. **Medium 3, hold delivery at dirty saturation.** Ordinary dirty receipts remain capped at 32. One additional receipt is reserved exclusively for the active hold. The hold owns the existing pending failure slot until accepted; a new failure or overflow that would replace it waits for a later intake. Pending capacity stays 63 ordinary entries plus one failure slot. The reservation never carries ordinary traffic. A control-generated hold does not need an app ACK before transport, so a failed ACK-file publication cannot hide its owned alert. All dirty receipts, including the hold, are flushed in acceptance order in one record. Total undurable receipts are bounded by 33. I-1 now bounds the replay suffix by 33, with at most 32 later accepted keys, still below 128. The extra receipt is the hold already counted by H in I-2, so its bound remains `96 + H`. A new hold episode cannot reuse the reservation until the prior completion is durable, which also flushes the prior receipts.

5. **Medium 1, exact R-loss-1 conditions.** Keep the wording exactly **"both processes end, together or one after the other"**. A distinct event E2 can be suppressed as E1 only when all of these hold:
   1. E1 and E2 have the same home, kind, and millisecond as sent.
   2. The CP accepted E1 and its exact key row is still retained when E2 arrives. This includes a row outside the newest 128 before the pruning sweep runs.
   3. The app held E1 only in memory, with no durable add, tombstone, or covering floor protecting that key.
   4. Control held E1 only in memory, with no durable journal record protecting that key.
   5. Both memory copies have ended at their respective key-choice steps: the old app ends before the new app chooses E2's key, and old control ends before new control admits E2. Thus both processes end, together or one after the other. The new app may choose E2 while old control is still alive; that control can end before its next intake of E2.

   The retained-but-not-newest sequence and the interleaved app/control ending sequence are explicit bound PINs. Suppression is the intended observation, not a defect to remove. Dropping retained membership after an actual sweep delivers the key. R-dup-2 refers to these conditions; no time limit or later ticket is introduced.

6. **Medium 6, qualification discriminators.** The built r8 already uses 300 later items for T17f, 512 durable candidates plus 32 accepted candidates for P25, an oldest-retained boundary for C7c, and a replacement inode without a relink fault for T19b. Preserve these stronger fixtures. T17f asserts the actual missing tombstone and covering floor before restart. C7c also checks all 128 retained rows and the exact boundary row. MU-83/MU-84, MU-78, MU-45, and MU-88 must each go RED alone. Restart fixtures now reconcile recovered entries before later writes; their exact delivery, key, and compaction assertions remain.

7. **Medium 7, D10 cadence.** D10 must reevaluate each observation while polling. Its 150-second delivery budget includes three intake probes with timeouts (30 seconds), the normal 60-second heartbeat cadence, lease/app/heartbeat calls (35 seconds), and 25 seconds of margin. This includes a hold beginning just after a heartbeat. D10 holds intake while creating a confirmed durable item, remounts the store read-only, then releases intake so the receipt produces a real failed retirement. Both abort requests use the existing route and a complete job payload. The bounded fixture must accept that deployment and reject a deployment that never posts the hold. Recovery must also be queried again until the count is zero. No live deployment is implied by the shell fixture.

8. **Low, H threshold.** Retention requires strictly fewer than 128 later keys. The bound `96 + H` ceases to prove retention at `H = 32`, because it then equals 128. This does not prove that the upper bound is attained. H counts newly generated hold items in W, not an unqualified number of restarts. With a refusing control journal, each process can add at most one such item in an uninterrupted unresolved window, including the initial process. Do not translate the threshold into "more than 32 restarts".

The final report distinguishes behavioral r8 REDs from existing r8 guards and intentional bound PINs. A passing r8 guard is never relabeled RED. The build is followed by a confirm-only review.
